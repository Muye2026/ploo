# Component Selection

Translate the product goal and architecture into credible, package-aware component candidates before industrial design, schematic, PCB, or CAD work.

## Research inputs

When current research is available, inspect a balanced mix of manufacturer data, distributor references, comparable products, teardowns, repair notes, maker builds, and mechanism examples. Mark price, stock, revision, dimensions, and lifecycle claims unverified unless checked in the current run.

## Required outputs

### Reference cases

Capture the observed architecture, useful lesson, design implication, source, and uncertainty for 3–6 relevant cases.

### Component requirements

For each critical module, capture function, target envelope, electrical or mechanical interface, mounting, environment, service, and industrial-design implications.

### Candidate components

Provide 2–4 plausible candidates for each material decision. Record approximate envelope, interface, mounting, visible impact, sourcing risk, and evidence.

### Selection gate

Do not choose the final critical component set autonomously. Recommend a set, explain trade-offs and downstream effects, and wait for the user's selection or explicit approval to use a named provisional set.

If no candidate satisfies a hard constraint, open a conflict gate before generating directions or implementation plans. Never hide the conflict by relaxing the constraint or substituting a component.

### Packaging constraints

Translate the user-approved or user-provisional set into thickness drivers, openings, service access, battery/cable/antenna/thermal keep-outs, mounting logic, and environmental risks.

### Sourcing risks

Record regional availability, uncertain dimensions, package variants, lifecycle, stock, thermal, durability, safety, and supplier-specific risks that could force redesign.
